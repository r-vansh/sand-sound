# Real-time simulation methods for sand reacting to vibration and sound (browser, plain JS + WebGL2)

Reading note: "Cited Findings" are things read in a source during this research session. "Inferences" include derivations, discretisations and pseudo-code written by the researcher from those findings plus standard numerics; they are not quoted from a source and have not been run. Items recalled from memory but not re-opened this session are flagged as such in "Gaps".

Notation: h = sand height, rho = areal sand density (equivalent to h for a thin layer), A(x) = local plate displacement amplitude, f = frequency, omega = 2*pi*f, Gamma = A*omega^2/g (dimensionless acceleration), D = grain diffusivity, dx = cell size, dt = time step.

## 1. How existing Chladni simulations move sand, and the continuum equivalent

### Takeaway
The physically supported model is a random walk whose step length grows with local vibration amplitude; its continuum limit is d(rho)/dt = laplacian(D(x) * rho) (flux J = -grad(D*rho), not Fick's -D*grad(rho)), which gives a steady state rho proportional to 1/D, so sand piles up on nodal lines. Most hobby simulations instead use gradient descent toward nodes plus jitter, which is the drift term of the same equation without the diffusion term.

### Cited Findings
- Grabec (Physics Letters A 381, 2017, pp. 59-64, "Vibration driven random walk in a Chladni experiment") proposed a purely statistical explanation: each grain is a random walker whose steps shorten as it nears a nodal line; Grabec and Sok confirmed numerically that many such walkers form a Chladni figure. — [Search summary of arXiv 2606.05913 / Grabec citations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05913)
- In Grabec's model the horizontal displacement per bounce is Gaussian and direction-independent, with standard deviation proportional to the local surface vibration amplitude, so the pattern can be simulated with a plain Gaussian random number generator. — [Grabec & Sok, SV-JME (dlib.si copy)](https://www.dlib.si/stream/URN:NBN:SI:doc-UD1CHCV8/f3cc8dc7-ac00-40fb-a205-ee5fd5c6e100/PDF)
- Grabec and Sok then treated pattern formation macroscopically as a diffusion process, deriving a generalised diffusion equation from the random-walk generator "following Einstein's treatment of Brownian motion". — [Grabec & Sok, SV-JME](https://ojs30.sv-jme.eu/index.php/sv-jme/article/download/507/217/5469)
- Abramian, Protiere, Lazarus, Devauchelle, "Chladni patterns explained by the space-dependent diffusion of bouncing grains", Phys. Rev. Research 7, L032001 (July 2025): grains are random walkers on a vibrated membrane, diffusivity increases with local vibration amplitude, grains gather where diffusivity is low; they measured D versus amplitude, proposed a diffusive flux from the measurements, and the steady-state distribution matches observation. (Abstract only was readable; equations not seen.) — [APS abstract](https://journals.aps.org/prresearch/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.7.L032001)
- Follow-up preprint "Thermodynamics of bouncing grains" (Devauchelle, Popovic, Szymczak, Abramian, Lazarus, arXiv 2606.05930, June 2026) writes the two candidate flux laws explicitly: Fick, j = -D grad(rho) (their Eq. 1), versus j = -grad(D rho) (Eq. 2, attributed to Buttiker and Landauer). Fick predicts a uniform density; Eq. 2 predicts rho proportional to 1/D (Eq. 3), specifically rho = <D>_h / (pi R^2 D) on a disk of radius R, with <D>_h the harmonic mean of D (Eq. 14). Their experiments support Eq. 2. — [arXiv 2606.05930](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05930)
- Same preprint, measured dependence: D = l^2/(4 tau) = v^3 tan(theta) / (2 g) (Eq. 10), where l is the hop length, tau the hop duration, v the grain velocity scale and theta the bounce angle; fitted exponent 3.3 +/- 0.5 and theta about 75 +/- 6 degrees. Hop duration tau = 2*sqrt(2h/g) for bounce height h (Eq. 6). — [arXiv 2606.05930](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05930)
- Same preprint, threshold: grains start bouncing only when plate vertical acceleration exceeds gravity, A*omega^2 >~ g; "If the dimensionless acceleration is less than one, the grains do not leave the plate, and the diffusivity vanishes." — [arXiv 2606.05930](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05930)
- Same preprint, magnitudes: disk radius 3.9 cm, drive 30-40 Hz, amplitude about 0.5 mm, grain sizes 1.5-6 mm, bounce height about 3 mm, grain rms speed about 20-26 cm/s, measured D from 0.9 to 20 cm^2/s depending on run and grain hardness. — [arXiv 2606.05930](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05930)
- luciopaiva/chladni (vanilla JS, canvas): uses the square-plate pattern function cos(n*pi*x/L)*cos(m*pi*y/L) - cos(m*pi*x/L)*cos(n*pi*y/L) (m == n gives no pattern); precomputes the field once per pattern, then builds a gradient field where each grid cell checks its 8 neighbours to pick the direction particles should move; particles "shake and move" along it toward nodal lines. Field + gradients for about 500k grid points take about 100 ms on an Intel i5-4670, so it is done in a Web Worker and the buffer is transferred (no copy). README gives no step sizes or particle count. — [luciopaiva/chladni](https://www.github.com/luciopaiva/chladni)
- Other open implementations: dmackinnon1/chladni (JS; particles settle where the surface moves least, adjustable vibrations), gregorybchris/chladni (TypeScript; random particle motion, no particle-particle interaction), ratwolfzero/Chladni_Figures (Python; field-based, no explicit particles, simplified membrane model with stochastic element). Code of these was not inspected. — [dmackinnon1](https://github.com/dmackinnon1/chladni); [gregorybchris listing](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fgregorybchris%2Fchladni); [ratwolfzero write-up](https://dev.to/ratwolf/fast-chladni-pattern-simulation-with-a-simplified-membrane-model-using-vectorization-and-stochastic-30gf)

### Inferences
- Derivation (researcher's, standard Ito/Fokker-Planck argument consistent with the cited Eq. 2). Let a grain at x hop every tau by a zero-mean isotropic Gaussian step with per-axis standard deviation sigma(x) evaluated at the departure point. Then P(x, t+tau) = integral of P(x - s, t) * G(s; sigma(x - s)) ds. Taylor-expanding in s to second order, the first-order term vanishes by symmetry and the second gives
  d(rho)/dt = laplacian( D(x) * rho ), with D(x) = sigma(x)^2 / (2 tau) per axis (equivalently l^2/(4 tau) for 2D hop length l, matching the cited Eq. 10 form).
  Expanding: J = -grad(D rho) = -D grad(rho) - rho grad(D). The second term is a drift with velocity u = -grad(D) toward low-D regions. With sigma = k*A (Grabec), D proportional to A^2 and u proportional to -grad(A^2): that drift is exactly what "gradient descent toward nodes" simulations implement; the first term is the "shake".
- Conflict to flag: the brief assumes D proportional to amplitude squared (follows from Grabec's sigma proportional to A). The measured law in arXiv 2606.05930 is D proportional to v^3 (exponent 3.3 +/- 0.5), i.e. roughly amplitude cubed at fixed frequency, with a hard cut-off D = 0 where Gamma < 1. For a visual experiment either works; a practical form covering both is D = D0 * max(0, Gamma_local - Gamma_c)^p with p between 2 and 3 and Gamma_c about 1.
- The Gamma < 1 cut-off is useful, not just a detail: it produces nodal bands of finite width (D = 0 inside them) instead of the singular rho proportional to 1/D pile at an exact node, and louder drive makes the bands narrower, as on a real plate.
- Conservative explicit scheme (this is literally the random walk on a grid). Define lambda_i = D_i * dt / dx^2. Each step, cell i sends the fraction lambda_i of its own sand to each of its 4 neighbours and keeps (1 - 4 lambda_i):
  ```
  rho_new[i] = rho[i] * (1 - n_i * lam[i]) + sum over neighbours j of ( lam[j] * rho[j] )
  // n_i = number of in-bounds neighbours (no-flux walls: just do not send off-grid)
  ```
  Properties: total mass conserved exactly (every outgoing amount appears as someone's incoming); rho stays non-negative iff 4*lam_max <= 1, i.e. dt <= dx^2 / (4 D_max). At exactly 4*lam = 1 the checkerboard mode has amplification factor 1 - 8*lam = -1 (undamped flicker), so use lam_max <= 0.2. The discrete steady state is lam_i * rho_i = const, i.e. rho proportional to 1/D, matching the cited continuum result.
- Convergence speed is set by the slow regions: time to drain an antinode region of width L is about L^2/D. Run several substeps per frame (each with lam_max about 0.2) rather than one large step. In grid units, k substeps move sand a typical distance of sqrt(2*k*lam) cells.
- Particle version for comparison (if a particle overlay is wanted): x += sigma(x) * gaussian(), sigma = k * A(x), no gradient lookup needed; clamp or reflect at the plate edge. Adding a small sigma_min keeps grains from freezing permanently.
- Do not discretise as div(D grad rho) (Fick form): the cited work states that this predicts uniform density and no pattern.

### Gaps
- The full text of Grabec 2017 and of Abramian et al. 2025 was not opened (paywall / abstract only); their exact coefficients (Grabec's proportionality constant, Abramian's fitted flux expression) are not recorded here.
- In arXiv 2606.05930 the "v" in D = v^3 tan(theta)/(2g) was reported by the extraction tool ambiguously as "plate vibration / grain speed"; it is most likely the grain rms velocity. Whether grain velocity is linear in plate velocity A*omega was not confirmed.

## 2. Height-field sand models and cheap mass-conserving update rules

### Takeaway
Three levels exist: (a) talus-threshold "thermal erosion" relaxation, the cheapest and what terrain tools and GPU libraries use; (b) nonlinear slope-dependent diffusion; (c) the two-layer BCRE model with a static height h and a rolling layer R exchanging mass at a rate that changes sign at the repose slope. All can be written as explicit flux exchanges between neighbouring cells, which conserves mass by construction.

### Cited Findings
- BCRE (Bouchaud, Cates, Ravi Prakash, Edwards) describes a pile by static height h(x,t) and rolling-layer thickness R(x,t). Simplified 1D form: d_t h = Gamma[h,R]; d_t R + d_x(v R) = w - Gamma[h,R], where w is an external source of rolling grains and v the downhill drift speed (constant in the original 1D model). — [Prigozhin & Zaltzman, "On two continuous models for the dynamics of sandpile surfaces", arXiv cond-mat/0005239](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0005239)
- The linearised BCRE exchange term is Gamma = gamma * R * (alpha_r - theta): rolling grains are deposited where slope angle theta is below the repose angle alpha_r and static grains are entrained where it is above. gamma has dimension 1/time. — [arXiv cond-mat/0005239](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0005239)
- A 2D form better suited to a grid (their modified model): d_t h = Gamma; d_t R + div(v R) = w - Gamma; drift velocity v = -mu * grad(h); exchange Gamma = gamma * R * (1 - |grad h|^2 / k^2), with k = tan(alpha_r). Characteristic lengths: rolling path L_P = mu/gamma, rolling-layer thickness L_R = w/gamma. Maximum-principle bounds: |grad h| <= k, m >= 0. — [arXiv cond-mat/0005239](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0005239)
- Another published variant of the exchange term includes curvature: Gamma = -R * [gamma * d_x(h~) + kappa * d_xx(h~)], h~ = h + x*tan(theta_r), gamma, kappa > 0. — [Search summary of Aradian, Raphael, de Gennes, "Surface flows of granular materials: a short introduction to some recent models", arXiv cond-mat/0112227](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0112227)
- The same paper notes the coupled system is stiff: the authors used an implicit finite-difference scheme and needed about 1e5 time steps in one example (nu = 0.01, lambda = 0.1*nu, k = 1). In the limit of a thin, fast rolling layer it reduces to a single conservative equation d_t h + div(q) = w with q = -m * grad(h). — [arXiv cond-mat/0005239](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0005239)
- BCRE-style two-layer (surface flowing layer + static layer) models have been used directly for graphics sand animation. — [Zhu & Yang, "Animating sand as a surface flow" (MIT CSAIL copy)](https://people.csail.mit.edu/boolzhu/papers/sand_surface_flow.pdf)
- Sumner, O'Brien, Hodgins, "Animating Sand, Mud, and Snow" (Graphics Interface 1998; Computer Graphics Forum 18(1), 1999, pp. 17-26): ground is a height field of vertical columns; on contact, material is compressed and displaced to neighbouring columns, then an erosion step relaxes slopes that are too steep. — [Eurographics DL record](https://diglib.eg.org/items/a18f4810-e22f-4f61-971f-e33e29d70ee0)
- GPU thermal erosion in Eric Galin's heightfield library: single-layer (or bedrock + sediment) height field, compute shader, ping-ponged sediment buffers; parameters are eps = 1.0 (quantity moved downhill per step) and tanThresholdAngle = 0.57 (about 30 degrees), optionally perturbed by a 3D noise so the threshold is not uniform; it exposes the last-step elevation difference to detect steady state. — [GPUThermalErosion class docs](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eric.galin/Doc/Heightfield/class_g_p_u_thermal_erosion.html)
- A CPU reference of the same idea (symbios-ground Rust crate): iteratively redistributes the excess above the talus angle from steep cells to their downhill neighbours; defaults 50 iterations. — [symbios-ground thermal.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/symbios-ground/0.3.0/source/src/erosion/thermal.rs)
- For conservative outflow on a grid, the reference pattern is the virtual-pipe model (Mei et al. 2007): store outflow per neighbour and scale all of a cell's outflows by a common factor so total outflow never exceeds what the cell holds. — [VMV 2024 paper building on it](https://diglib.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/vmv20241211/vmv20241211.pdf)

### Inferences
- Talus rule in conservative per-edge form (researcher's formulation; symmetric, so it conserves mass and suits both CPU and gather-style shaders):
  ```
  // for each edge between cell i and neighbour j (distance d = dx, or dx*sqrt2 for diagonals)
  excess = (h[i] - h[j]) - talus * d          // talus = tan(angle of repose)
  q_ij   = rate * max(excess, 0)              // sand moved i -> j this step
  h_new[i] = h[i] - sum_j q_ij + sum_j q_ji
  ```
  Stability: with 4 neighbours use rate <= 0.25 (0.125 with 8) so a cell can never overshoot below its lowest neighbour; rate = 0.5 on a single pair is the exact "equalise to talus" step. Dry sand repose angle about 30-35 degrees, so talus about 0.57-0.70 in physical units; in a top-down toy, talus should be expressed in height units per cell and exposed as a knob.
  Limiter: if h is height above a rigid floor, scale all q_ij of a cell by min(1, h[i] / sum_j q_ij) (virtual-pipe style) so h never goes negative.
- 4-neighbour talus relaxation leaves visible diamond/axis-aligned artefacts (piles become pyramids); 8 neighbours with the sqrt2 distance correction, or a small noise on the threshold (as the Galin library does), hides this.
- BCRE on a grid (researcher's discretisation of the cited 2D equations; two scalar fields h and R):
  ```
  slope2 = |grad h|^2 (central differences)
  G = gamma * R * (1 - slope2 / k^2)          // >0 deposit, <0 entrain
  G = clamp(G, -h/dt, R/dt)                   // cannot entrain more than h, nor deposit more than R
  h += dt * G
  R += dt * (w - G)
  // advect R downhill conservatively: upwind flux on each edge
  F_ij = mu * max(h_i + R_i - h_j - R_j, 0) / dx * R_i * dt / dx
  scale so sum_j F_ij <= R_i ; R_i -= sum_j F_ij ; R_j += F_ij
  ```
  h + R summed over the grid changes only by the source w. Explicit stability needs mu * max_slope * dt / dx <= about 0.25 and gamma * dt < 1. The cited stiffness warning (1e5 implicit steps) applies to the thin-fast-layer limit; choosing gamma and mu so the rolling path L_P = mu/gamma is a few cells keeps an explicit scheme usable.
- BCRE has one weakness for this use: Gamma is proportional to R, so with R = 0 nothing ever starts moving even on an over-steep slope. A source term w is required; vibration is the natural source (see section 3).
- Cost ranking per step per cell: talus rule (4-8 neighbour reads, one field) < nonlinear diffusion (same reads, one divide) < BCRE (two fields, two passes). For a first version the talus rule plus the Chladni transport of section 1 is sufficient; BCRE adds visible avalanching fronts.

## 3. Vibration as local fluidisation: lowering the effective angle of repose

### Takeaway
There is a measured, one-parameter continuum law for exactly this: under vertical vibration a pile relaxes by nonlinear diffusion with diffusivity proportional to the plate's vibration velocity and to the local sand depth, diverging as the slope approaches the repose slope. Below Gamma about 1-2 nothing moves, so ridges on nodal lines persist while ridges in shaken regions slump.

### Cited Findings
- Tsuji, Otsuki, Katsuragi, "Relaxation Dynamics of a Granular Pile on a Vertically-Vibrating Plate" (arXiv 1708.03752) propose the nonlinear diffusion transport (NDT) model. Mean downslope transport velocity: v_t = (c * v_vib / mu^2) * |grad h| / (1 - (|grad h| / mu)^2), with v_vib = A * (2*pi*f) the plate's peak velocity, mu = tan(theta_c) the friction coefficient at the repose angle, and c the "relaxation efficiency" (fraction of vibration energy converted into horizontal transport). Flux is q = h * v_t. — [arXiv 1708.03752](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.03752)
- Resulting evolution equation: d_t h = div( h * (c * v_vib / mu^2) * grad(h) / (1 - (|grad h| / mu)^2) ). It reduces to Fick's law for |grad h| << mu and the flux diverges as |grad h| -> mu. — [arXiv 1708.03752](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.03752)
- Measured values: c = 0.068 +/- 0.014, constant across all conditions; mu = 0.45-0.65 depending on grains; a simple Fickian law (q proportional to slope only) does not fit because q also scales with depth h. — [arXiv 1708.03752](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.03752)
- Conditions: f = 50-500 Hz, grain diameter 0.5 or 1.0 mm, pile radius 40 mm, 2 <= Gamma <= 10; onset of relaxation Gamma_c lies between 1 and 2 depending on frequency; model stated valid for roughly v_vib = 10-200 mm/s and fails near jamming (weak vibration) and in the granular-gas regime. Their own explicit simulation used dt = 1e-5 s at 0.5 mm resolution. — [arXiv 1708.03752](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1708.03752)
- Qualitative regimes from earlier experiments: under strong vibration the pile angle decays toward zero roughly logarithmically in time; gently vibrated piles drop a little and then stay jammed at a finite angle. Creep regions are reported even in dense vibrated beds at Gamma >= 2. — [Search summary of arXiv cond-mat/0407201 and related](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0407201); [arXiv 1902.02942](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.02942v3)
- A granular pile is metastable below the repose angle; vibration relaxes its structure. — [arXiv 1708.03752](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03752)

### Inferences
- Drop-in local version: make v_vib a field. With the mode model of section 4, v_vib(x) = sqrt(2 * sum_k E_k * phi_k(x)^2) * omega (rms plate velocity), then
  K(x) = c * v_vib(x) * h / mu^2 * 1 / (1 - min(s/mu, 0.95)^2), with s = |grad h|, and d_t h = div(K grad h).
  Clamping s/mu at 0.95 caps the nonlinear factor at about 10 and keeps the explicit step bounded; gate with a smooth step on Gamma(x) around 1-2 so sand in quiet zones is frozen.
- Conservative explicit discretisation: evaluate K on edges as the average (or the upwind/uphill value) of the two cells, flux q_ij = K_edge * (h_i - h_j) * dt / dx^2 from the higher to the lower cell; stable when 4 * K_max * dt / dx^2 <= 1 (use <= 0.8). Because K is proportional to h, flux vanishes automatically where there is no sand, so no negativity limiter is needed if the uphill cell's h is used.
- Cheaper stylised alternative: keep the talus rule of section 2 and make the threshold a function of shaking, talus_eff(x) = talus_0 * (1 - a * F(x)), F = saturate((Gamma(x) - 1) / (Gamma_fluid - 1)), with a near 1 so strongly shaken sand flattens completely (consistent with the cited "angle decays toward zero under strong vibration") and still sand keeps talus_0. The rate constant can also scale with v_vib so collapse speed follows loudness.
- Coupling to BCRE: use vibration as the entrainment source w in the rolling-layer equation, w(x) = e * max(Gamma(x) - 1, 0) * (available h), and let the rolling layer move by the section-1 operator laplacian(D R) in addition to downhill drift. This gives one consistent picture: shaking lifts grains into a mobile layer, the mobile layer random-walks (collecting where D is small) and rolls downhill, and it re-deposits where the surface is still and below repose.
- Combined single-field recipe that is probably enough visually: per substep apply (1) Chladni transport rho <- laplacian(D rho) on the top "mobile" portion of the column, (2) NDT or shaken-talus slumping, (3) plain talus limit at talus_0 everywhere as a safety net. All three are edge-flux updates and can share one shader pass.
- Physical-unit sanity check for parameter defaults: a 300 Hz mode needs A >= g / omega^2, about 2.8 micrometres, to reach Gamma = 1 (v_vib about 5 mm/s); the NDT validity window v_vib = 10-200 mm/s then corresponds to Gamma about 2-40 at that frequency. In a toy, work in dimensionless Gamma and tune by eye.

### Gaps
- No source was found giving a closed-form effective repose angle as a function of Gamma; the talus_eff(Gamma) mapping above is a design choice, not a measured law.
- The NDT model was validated for a pile on a uniformly vibrating plate; no source applies it with a spatially varying v_vib (the Chladni case). Using it locally is an extrapolation.
- No graphics or game reference was found that models vibration-dependent repose angle on a height field.

## 4. Mapping live audio (AnalyserNode FFT) onto plate modes

### Takeaway
No existing open project was found that drives a bank of damped modal resonators from a live spectrum and feeds a sand transport model; existing audio-reactive "cymatics" demos map audio to pattern parameters heuristically. The physically motivated construction (modal superposition, time-averaged squared amplitude as an energy-weighted sum of squared mode shapes) therefore has to be assembled from standard vibration theory.

### Cited Findings
- cos*cos mode shapes with frequencies omega_mn = (pi*c/a) * sqrt(m^2 + n^2) are exact for a square membrane, and are a simplification of a real elastic plate, whose modes are eigenfunctions of the biharmonic operator with free edges. Plain cosine products do not satisfy free-edge plate conditions; Ritz (1909) made the first accurate computation of square-plate Chladni figures, and Gander & Wanner's treatment uses a basis mixing cos and cosh terms (roots of tanh k + tan k = 0). — [ratwolfzero Chladni write-up](https://dev.to/ratwolf/fast-chladni-pattern-simulation-with-a-simplified-membrane-model-using-vectorization-and-stochastic-30gf); [Gander & Kwok, "Chladni figures and the Tacoma bridge" (PDF)](https://giref.ulaval.ca/~fkwok/docs/chladni-print.pdf); [Georgi, Physics of Waves 11.3](https://phys.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Waves_and_Acoustics/The_Physics_of_Waves_(Goergi)/11%3A_Two_and_Three_Dimensions/11.03%3A_Chladni_Plates)
- The formula most web simulations use for a centre-driven square plate is the antisymmetric/symmetric combination cos(n*pi*x/L)cos(m*pi*y/L) -/+ cos(m*pi*x/L)cos(n*pi*y/L). — [luciopaiva/chladni](https://www.github.com/luciopaiva/chladni)
- Audio-reactive projects found (code not inspected): Observable notebooks by "inoculate" titled "WebGL Chladni" and "Microphone Audio Chladni Patterns"; Wavefield (three.js, real-time audio visualiser that started from Chladni references and is explicitly not physically accurate); a Max/Jitter forum thread on audio-reactive Chladni plates; didgety's Chladni plate simulation blog post; klmntv/chladni (p5.js). — [Observable @inoculate](https://observablehq.com/@inoculate?page=2); [Wavefield](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/wavefield-real-time-immersive-audio-visualizer/92098); [Cycling74 thread](https://cycling74.com/forums/chladni-plates-audio-reactive-simulation); [didgety blog](https://didgety.github.io/blog/2023/chladni_plate_simulation); [klmntv/chladni](https://github.com/klmntv/chladni)
- Physical thresholds that the audio mapping must respect: grains only move where A*omega^2 exceeds about g, and diffusivity rises steeply (about cubic) with vibration velocity above that. — [arXiv 2606.05930](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05930)

### Inferences
(Everything below is standard linear-vibration theory and Web Audio usage written from the researcher's knowledge; not quoted from a fetched source.)
- Mode bank. Choose N modes (16-40 is plenty) with shapes phi_k(x,y) and natural frequencies f_k. Cheap shapes: phi_mn = cos(m*pi*x)cos(n*pi*y) +/- cos(n*pi*x)cos(m*pi*y) on [0,1]^2 (antinodes at the free edges, which looks right). Frequencies: thin-plate bending scaling f_mn = f_0 * (m^2 + n^2) (plate, stiffness-dominated) rather than the membrane sqrt(m^2 + n^2); f_0 is a free "plate size/thickness" knob that places the low modes in the 80-200 Hz range so music and voice excite many modes.
- Each mode as a damped resonator: q_k'' + (omega_k / Q_k) q_k' + omega_k^2 q_k = F_k(t). Steady-state power gain at drive frequency f: |H_k(f)|^2 = 1 / ( (1 - (f/f_k)^2)^2 + (f / (Q_k f_k))^2 ). Half-power bandwidth is f_k / Q_k. Q about 20-100 looks plate-like; lower Q (5-15) gives a more forgiving, always-moving toy.
- Drive from the FFT. With AnalyserNode: bin width = sampleRate / fftSize (about 23.4 Hz at 48 kHz, fftSize 2048); getFloatFrequencyData returns dB, convert with P_b = 10^(dB/10). Target modal energy P_k = g_k * sum_b P_b * |H_k(f_b)|^2, where g_k is the coupling of the driver to mode k (phi_k at the drive point squared; for a centre drive, modes with a node at the centre get g_k = 0, which is why centre-driven plates show only the symmetric family). Precompute the weight matrix W[k][b] = g_k * |H_k(f_b)|^2 once; per frame it is an N x (number of bins) dot product, trivial on the CPU.
- Ring-up and ring-down. Do not let the mode energy jump: integrate dE_k/dt = (P_k - E_k) / tau_k with tau_k = Q_k / (2*pi*f_k) (energy decay time of a resonator; amplitude decays with twice that). For Q = 50 at 200 Hz that is about 40 ms, shorter than a frame or two, so in practice add a user "sustain" multiplier. Set analyser.smoothingTimeConstant to 0 (default is 0.8) so smoothing is done once, by the mode model.
- Time-averaged field. Plate displacement w(x,t) = sum_k a_k phi_k(x) cos(omega_k t + p_k). Over times long compared with beat periods, cross terms between modes of different frequency average to zero, so <w^2>(x) = (1/2) sum_k a_k^2 phi_k(x)^2 = sum_k E_k phi_k(x)^2. This is the field to feed the transport model; nodes of the summed field are where every excited mode is simultaneously near zero, so broadband sound gives blurrier figures and a pure tone gives a crisp one, as in reality. Degenerate modes (same f_k, e.g. (m,n) and (n,m)) do not decorrelate: combine them coherently into one shape (the +/- combination above) before squaring.
- What drives sand is acceleration, not displacement: Gamma(x)^2 proportional to sum_k E_k * omega_k^4 * phi_k(x)^2 (and velocity squared uses omega_k^2). High modes therefore need far less displacement to move sand. For a toy, either keep the omega weighting and compress dynamics with a global auto-gain, or drop it and treat E_k directly as "shaking strength".
- From field to transport coefficients: S(x) = sum_k E_k w_k phi_k(x)^2 (w_k = 1, omega_k^2 or omega_k^4), Gamma(x) = gain * sqrt(S), D(x) = D0 * max(0, Gamma - 1)^p (p = 2 to 3), then lam = min(0.2, D * dt / dx^2). An automatic gain that maps the running peak of S to a chosen Gamma_max (for example 3-6) keeps behaviour similar at different input volumes.
- Evaluation cost: sum over N modes of two cosines per pixel is fine in a fragment shader (N <= 32) rendered into an R16F/R32F "D texture" once per frame, or at half resolution; alternatively precompute phi_k^2 into texture array layers and blend. On the CPU, separability helps: cos(m*pi*x) tables per axis make each mode two lookups and a multiply per cell.
- Time-domain alternative: feed the raw signal through N biquad band-pass filters (BiquadFilterNode type "bandpass", frequency f_k, Q_k) each followed by its own AnalyserNode or a single AudioWorklet computing rms. This is a truer resonator bank with better low-frequency resolution than the FFT (whose 23 Hz bins are coarse relative to low-mode spacing), at the cost of more nodes.

## 5. GPU (WebGL2 ping-pong float textures) versus CPU typed arrays

### Takeaway
Both are feasible at 300k-500k cells; WebGL2 with EXT_color_buffer_float and R32F/RGBA16F ping-pong targets is the safer route to 60 fps with several substeps per frame, provided the update is written in gather form so mass is conserved. The CPU route is simpler to debug and is plausible at 512 x 512 with a few substeps.

### Cited Findings
- EXT_color_buffer_float is WebGL2-only and makes these formats colour-renderable (framebuffer-complete as attachments, accepted by renderbufferStorage): R16F, RG16F, RGBA16F, R32F, RG32F, RGBA32F, R11F_G11F_B10F. Without it, float textures cannot be render targets. — [MDN: EXT_color_buffer_float](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/EXT_color_buffer_float)
- Float blending and linear filtering of float textures are separate extensions (EXT_float_blend, OES_texture_float_linear); the EXT_color_buffer_float page does not grant them. — [MDN: EXT_color_buffer_float](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/EXT_color_buffer_float)
- A real GPU height-field erosion implementation uses exactly the ping-pong layout: input sediment buffer, output sediment buffer, swap, n steps per call, optional restriction of steps to a circular region, and a "last step elevation difference" readout for convergence. — [Galin GPUThermalErosion docs](https://perso.liris.cnrs.fr/eric.galin/Doc/Heightfield/class_g_p_u_thermal_erosion.html)
- CPU reference point: in luciopaiva/chladni, evaluating the pattern (trig per cell) plus an 8-neighbour gradient for about 500k cells costs about 100 ms on an i5-4670 in plain JS, i.e. far too slow per frame, which is why it runs in a worker once per pattern. — [luciopaiva/chladni](https://www.github.com/luciopaiva/chladni)

### Inferences
- Gather form is what makes a fragment shader conservative. A fragment can only write its own cell, so define one pure function out(i -> j) (depends only on the old state of cells i and j and the static coefficient textures) and compute, for each cell, new = old - sum_j out(self -> j) + sum_j out(j -> self). Because the same function is evaluated with the same inputs on both sides of an edge, what one cell loses another gains, up to float rounding. For the section-1 transport this is the single line
  `new = c*(1.0 - n*lamC) + lamL*l + lamR*r + lamU*u + lamD*d;`
  For limiter-based rules (talus with a "cannot go negative" scale factor), the neighbour's scale factor depends on the neighbour's neighbours (a 2-cell radius). Either do two passes (pass 1 writes the 4 outflows into an RGBA texture, pass 2 sums them), which is the virtual-pipe layout, or choose rates that make the limiter unnecessary (rate <= 0.25 with 4 neighbours).
- Use texelFetch(sampler, ivec2, 0) with NEAREST filtering and CLAMP_TO_EDGE; handle walls explicitly (skip out-of-range neighbours) so nothing leaks at the border. This also avoids needing OES_texture_float_linear on the simulation textures (the display pass can do its own bilinear/bicubic interpolation manually).
- Precision: prefer R32F (or RGBA32F if packing h, R, and extras) for the state. Half floats have about 11 bits of mantissa, so repeatedly adding small fluxes to a large height stalls (flux smaller than half an ulp is lost) and total mass drifts. If RGBA16F must be used (some mobile GPUs), keep heights in a small numeric range and fluxes not too small. Check gl.getExtension('EXT_color_buffer_float') and fall back to the CPU path if it is null.
- Mass audit on the GPU: reading back a full float texture every frame stalls the pipeline; instead downsample-sum into a tiny target occasionally (or readPixels every few seconds) and apply a uniform multiplicative correction if drift exceeds a tolerance.
- Texture layout suggestion: state A/B (R32F or RG32F: h and rolling layer R), coefficient texture (RG16F: lambda/D and shaking factor F, re-rendered once per frame from the mode energies uniform array), display pass reading state. Substeps = repeated draw of a full-screen triangle with FBO swap; 8-16 substeps per frame at 512^2-768^2 is a small fill-rate load for an integrated GPU (researcher's estimate, not measured).
- Per-frame pass budget: 1 coefficient pass (N modes of trig per pixel; do at half resolution if needed) + k transport passes (5-9 texel fetches each) + 1 render pass (the shadow march of section 6 is the most expensive part: steps x fetches per pixel).
- CPU route: Float32Array of 512 x 512 = 262k cells (or 704 x 704 = 496k). A 5-point update is a handful of flops per cell; a tight loop over typed arrays should take on the order of 1-3 ms per pass on a laptop (estimate, not measured), so 2-4 substeps per frame are plausible, 16 are not. Keep trig out of the inner loop (precompute per-axis cosine tables; update D only when mode energies change appreciably, or amortise over frames). Upload h each frame with texSubImage2D to an R32F texture for the lit rendering pass; that upload at 512^2 is cheap. A Web Worker with a transferred buffer (as luciopaiva does) keeps the UI responsive but adds a frame of latency.
- Recommendation given "single file, 60 fps, laptop": GPU simulation with a CPU fallback is unnecessary complexity for a first version; pick GPU if several substeps and a 600-700 px grid are wanted, CPU if 512^2 with 1-3 substeps is acceptable. Audio analysis and the mode-energy integration stay on the CPU in both cases.

### Gaps
- No benchmark source was found for WebGL2 float ping-pong at these sizes or for JS typed-array stencil throughput; all performance numbers above other than the luciopaiva figure are estimates.
- The Khronos extension registry text (precise rules on float blending, RGBA32F support on mobile, and implicit enabling of related extensions) was not fetched; only the MDN summary was read.
- No source was found discussing conservation/precision pitfalls of half-float simulation textures specifically.

## 6. Rendering granular sand from a height field in a fragment shader

### Takeaway
The best-documented real-time recipe is Journey's (John Edwards, GDC 2013, as reconstructed by Alan Zucconi): a high-contrast modified Lambert term, per-grain normal perturbation from a random-vector texture, a broad Blinn-Phong "ocean" specular plus rim term, and view-dependent glitter from per-grain mirror facets. For a top-down height field this is combined with a normal from the height gradient and a low-angle light; cast shadows by height-field ray marching are a standard addition but were not sourced this session.

### Cited Findings
- Journey's diffuse ("diffuse contrast") model: I = saturate(4 * dot(N * (1, 0.3, 1), L)), then colour = lerp(shadowColor, terrainColor, I). The factor 4 makes anything receiving 25% of full Lambert light fully lit (hard terminator, strong contrast); scaling the normal's vertical component by 0.3 exaggerates the response to horizontal tilt. Found by trial and error for a recognisable look rather than physical accuracy. — [Zucconi, Journey Sand Shader part 2: Diffuse Colour](https://www.alanzucconi.com/?p=10052)
- Grain normal: sample a random-vector texture at grain scale, S = normalize(rand.rgb * 2 - 1), then tilt the surface normal toward it: N' = normalize(lerp(N, S, sandStrength)) (nlerp is used instead of slerp for cost). — [Zucconi part 3: Sand Normal](https://www.alanzucconi.com/2019/10/08/journey-sand-shader-3/)
- Journey's random texture was generated from a Gaussian distribution so the dominant direction stays aligned with the surface normal. — [Zucconi series, via search summary](https://www.alanzucconi.com/?p=10054)
- Rim term: rim = saturate(pow(1 - saturate(dot(N, V)), rimPower) * rimStrength) * rimColor. "Ocean specular" (Edwards' term; the sand was meant to read as a fluid, like low sun on water): Blinn-Phong, H = normalize(V + L), spec = pow(max(0, dot(N, H)), power) * strength * color. The two are combined with max(), not added, so overlapping areas do not get too shiny: color = diffuse + saturate(max(rim, ocean)). No numeric constants are published for these. — [Zucconi part 4: Specular Reflection](https://www.alanzucconi.com/?p=10057)
- Glitter: treat each grain as a tiny mirror with a random normal G from a noise texture; R = reflect(L, G); the grain sparkles only when R is almost exactly aligned with the view vector V (thresholded dot(R, V)), which makes it rare and view/light dependent, and using reflect (rather than a fresh random value) keeps it temporally stable as light or camera move. Glitter intensity is pushed above 1 so bloom can spread it. Threshold and colour values are not published. — [Zucconi part 5: Glitter Reflection](https://www.alanzucconi.com/?p=10059)
- Journey first tried ordinary specular for glitter and found the sparkle points too spread out; sparkles came from blending a noise normal map with the sand's base normal. Small ripples used four tiling height-map tiles blended by dune orientation/steepness over a low-resolution dune height map. — [Blog summary of the GDC talk](https://worldweavertagdb.blogspot.com/2013/11/seas-of-sand.html); [Polycount thread](https://polycount.com/discussion/125544/the-technical-art-behind-journey); [Zucconi part 6 pointer](https://www.alanzucconi.com/?p=10061)
- The primary source is the GDC 2013 session "Sand Rendering in Journey" (GDC Vault, login-gated) and the SIGGRAPH 2012 Advances in Real-Time Rendering course listing. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1017742/Sand-Rendering-in); [Advances in Real-Time Rendering 2012](https://www.realtimerendering.com/advances/s2012/index.html)

### Inferences
(Shader construction below is the researcher's, assembled from the cited Journey terms plus standard height-field techniques; unverified by running.)
- Normal from height: with texel size t and height scale z, N = normalize(vec3(-(hR - hL) * z / (2t), -(hU - hD) * z / (2t), 1)). Central differences on a NEAREST-sampled sim texture look faceted when magnified; fetch the four neighbours and interpolate manually (bilinear, or a 3x3 Sobel) or render at simulation resolution 1:1.
- Light: a low sun, elevation 15-30 degrees, makes millimetre-scale relief readable from directly above; expose azimuth/elevation. For a strictly top-down view V = (0, 0, 1), so the rim term is just a function of slope (steep flanks brighten) and the half-vector is fixed for a directional light, which makes the "ocean specular" a soft highlight on faces tilted toward the light.
- Adapting Journey's diffuse to z-up: scale the normal's vertical (z) component by about 0.3 before the dot product and multiply by 4, then saturate and lerp between a shadow tint and the sand colour. This gives the crisp lit/shadow split typical of raked sand. A more neutral alternative is Oren-Nayar with high roughness (sigma about 0.5-0.6 rad), which flattens the falloff and brightens grazing-lit rims compared with Lambert; for V = (0,0,1) it simplifies considerably. Either can be offered behind a toggle.
- Cast shadows by height-field ray marching (standard technique): from the pixel's (uv, h) step toward the light in uv by a fixed stride; at step i the ray height is h0 + i * stride * tan(elevation) / z; track occl = max(occl, (h_sample - h_ray) / (i * stride)) and shade = 1 - saturate(occl * softness). 16-32 steps with a per-pixel jittered start (interleaved gradient or hash noise) hide banding; stop when the ray exceeds the maximum bed height. Cost is steps x one fetch per pixel, usually the single most expensive part of the frame; it can be rendered at half resolution or into a separate R8 texture and blurred.
- Per-grain texture without an image file: hash the integer grain coordinate floor(uv * grainsPerUnit) to a pseudo-random unit vector (two hashes to spherical coordinates, biased toward +z to imitate the cited Gaussian-around-normal texture), use it for both the nlerp normal perturbation (strength about 0.1-0.3) and glitter. Grain cell size about 1-1.5 screen pixels avoids shimmer; apply at device-pixel scale, not simulation-texel scale.
- Glitter top-down: g = pow(saturate(dot(reflect(-L, G), V)), 200-500) or a hard threshold near 0.99+, multiplied by a second low-frequency hash so only a fraction of grains can ever sparkle. In a static top-down view nothing changes unless the light moves, so sparkles only twinkle where sand moves; tying a slight light wobble or the grain hash offset to local shaking strength makes vibrating zones shimmer, which reads as agitation.
- Extra cues that sell granularity cheaply: darken slightly by depth of sand so the bare plate shows through thin areas (alpha = saturate(h / h_thin)); a height-based ambient term (ridges lighter, troughs darker, approximating ambient occlusion with a wide blur of h); slight colour variation per grain (a few percent luminance noise).
- Motion blur of bouncing grains: in strongly shaken regions real sand looks hazy and lighter. Blend toward a brighter, lower-contrast, noisier shading by the shaking factor F(x) from section 3.
